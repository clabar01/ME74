Signal vs true distance

Dots are packets that arrived with a GPS fix, rings are calibration points, lines are the current model for each node. If the dots don't follow the lines, recalibrate.

Why calibrate

The dashboard turns signal strength into distance with two numbers per node: the signal at 1 m and how fast it fades (n). Every site is different, so measure them where you fly.

Option 1: tape measure

Hold the drone payload at a measured distance from a node, then press Collect. Do at least two distances (for example 2, 5, 10 and 20 m).

Option 2: from a GPS flight

Fly a drone that sends both radio and GPS around the nodes at a mix of distances, then fit. Every packet with a fresh GPS fix is used.

Math settings

How the math works

1. Node positions are turned into east/north meters around the first node.

2. Distance from signal: d = 10^((RSSI@1m − RSSI) / (10·n)), using the median of the last N packets.

3. That distance is a slant line up to the drone, so the flight height is used to get the distance along the ground.

4. Two nodes: the two distance circles cross at two points, one on each side of the line between the nodes. The drone's "side" setting picks one, then it follows the track. Three or more nodes: a least-squares fit with no mirror problem.

5. The accuracy circle comes from how the circles cross (bad when the drone is near the node line) and the signal noise. A Kalman filter smooths the track, then the result is turned back into latitude and longitude.
